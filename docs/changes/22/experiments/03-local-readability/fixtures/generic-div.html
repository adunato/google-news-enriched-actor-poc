Transit study finds safer crossings
By Staff Reporter

A new transit study found that redesigned street crossings reduced collisions near busy bus stops. Researchers reviewed three years of traffic records and interviewed passengers at twelve locations.

The report recommends wider refuge islands, clearer signal timing and brighter signs. It says the changes should be tested with people who walk, cycle and use mobility devices before construction starts.

Transport officials will review the findings this month. They have not selected locations or set a construction date, and the study does not estimate the cost of a citywide program.

Passenger groups welcomed the focus on safer access but asked for the agency to publish its data. The research team plans to release an anonymized dataset with its final report.
